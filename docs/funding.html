Funding

Who pays for global catastrophic risk work?

I collected every grant that four philanthropic funders publish: Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy), the Survival and Flourishing Fund, the Long-Term Future Fund and the Future of Life Institute. By default you see GCR outside AI and bio. That's three topics: nuclear, natural & resilience (nuclear war, volcanic eruptions, asteroids, space weather and keeping food systems and infrastructure running in a catastrophe), general x-risk (work on catastrophic or existential risk as a whole instead of a single hazard) and climate & geoengineering. The biggest part of general x-risk is FHI's core funding (almost half), followed by Global Shield's all-hazards policy work and student programmes like SERI and ERA. What the topics mean

GCR outside AI and bio is the first three topics below together: nuclear, natural & resilience, general x-risk, and climate & geoengineering. It only counts work framed as global catastrophic risk. Mainstream climate funding, nuclear security work and disaster risk reduction are much larger, but they are not what I am looking at here.

Nuclear, natural & resilience
Nuclear war, volcanoes, asteroids and comets, space weather, and keeping food systems and infrastructure running in a catastrophe. In practice, almost all of the money in 2015–2025 went to research on what a nuclear war would do to climate and crops (nuclear winter) and on resilient food. These four funders gave next to nothing for preventing nuclear war (one $0.5M regrant) and nothing for volcanoes.
AI
AI safety, alignment and AI governance.
Bio
Biosecurity and pandemic preparedness.
General x-risk
Catastrophic or existential risk as a whole, without a single hazard. For example multi-hazard institutes (FHI, GCRI), all-hazards policy (Global Shield) and student or fellowship programmes on x-risk (SERI, ERA, CERI).
Climate & geoengineering
Extreme climate change and research or governance of geoengineering.
Forecasting
Forecasting platforms and research meant to inform decisions on these risks.
EA & community
Effective altruism community building and infrastructure. Only shown in the "Everything" view.
Other
Grants I couldn't place from the public description, and, in the "Everything" view, grants that are not about catastrophic risk at all.

All amounts are nominal USD. SFF figures are recommendations, not confirmed payouts, and matching pledges are left out. LTFF and FLI publish only part of their grants, so their totals are too low. 2026* runs only to November. Money passed on between these funders (regrants) is counted once. Grants split between two topics (like 50 % AI and 50 % bio) have a * after the amount. Everything here is based on public data. The data and how I classified it are on GitHub.
