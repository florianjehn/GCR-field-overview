Global catastrophic risk research

How is the field doing?

I wanted to know how the field working on global catastrophic risk changed over the last ten years. Who pays for it, how many people work on it and how much gets written about it. My impression was that most of the money moved to AI and that the classic topics (nuclear war, volcanoes, space weather, keeping people fed in a catastrophe) are left with very little. This site collects the numbers to check that. Everything is based on public data and links back to its source, so you can check it yourself.

How this was made

I sorted the grants by topic and kind of work using the funders' own labels, simple rules and coding by hand (some of it with an LLM). Whenever a case was unclear, I decided it myself. Each grant records who coded it and why. The full method, the log of my decisions and a sensitivity analysis (how much the numbers move if you decide differently) are in the repository.

The main caveats. I only cover four philanthropic funders and no government money. LTFF and FLI publish only part of their grants, and SFF figures are recommendations. Team pages are not payrolls. The count of researchers is rough. It only covers people at the bigger, well-known organizations. People at smaller groups, in university departments or working independently aren't in it, so the real number is likely higher. On the other hand, everyone on a team page counts as one person, even if they work part-time. CSER researchers who mainly work on AI or bio are left out. Publications only count the exact term "global catastrophic risk". All amounts are nominal USD.
