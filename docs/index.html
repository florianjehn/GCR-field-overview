Global catastrophic risk research

How is the field doing?

I wanted to know how the field working on global catastrophic risk changed over the last ten years when it comes to funding and the people working on it. My impression was that most of the money moved to AI and bio, and that everything else is left with scraps. This site collects the numbers from the main funders and organizations and lets you explore them interactively. Everything is based on public data.

By "GCR outside AI and bio" I mean three things:

  • Abrupt sunlight reduction & infrastructure collapse: catastrophes that would block out the sun or knock out infrastructure over large areas, like nuclear war, large volcanic eruptions, asteroid impacts and severe space weather, and keeping food systems and infrastructure running through them.
  • General x-risk: work on catastrophic or existential risk as a whole, like FHI or Global Shield.
  • Climate & geoengineering.

I only count work framed as global catastrophic risk. Mainstream climate science, nuclear security and disaster risk reduction get far more money, but they look at these problems from a different point of view.

How this was made

I sorted the grants by topic and kind of work using the funders' own labels. Where those were not available, I let Claude classify the grant. Whenever a case was unclear to Claude, I decided it myself. The details are in the repository.

The main caveats. I only cover four philanthropic funders. LTFF and FLI publish only part of their grants, and SFF figures are recommendations. The count of researchers is rough and likely inaccurate. It only covers people at the bigger, well-known organizations. Team pages are also often out of date. They keep people who have left and miss new hires for a while, so the count can be off in both directions. People at these organizations who mainly work on AI or bio are left out. Publications only count the exact term "global catastrophic risk". All amounts are nominal USD.
